# RFNBO Rules and Exemptions – Legal Summary (June 2026)

## 1. Legal Basis

The key legal instrument is:

* **Commission Delegated Regulation (EU) 2023/1184** supplementing RED II/RED III by establishing the methodology for determining when electricity used to produce RFNBOs (primarily renewable hydrogen) can be counted as renewable.

The framework is built around three pillars:

1. **Additionality**
2. **Temporal correlation**
3. **Geographical correlation**

The purpose is to ensure that hydrogen production does not increase fossil electricity generation and contributes to additional renewable deployment.

---

# 2. Additionality

## 2.1 Core Principle

Hydrogen producers connected to the grid must demonstrate that the renewable electricity claimed for RFNBO production comes from **new renewable generation capacity**.

Article 5 states:

> "The installation generating renewable electricity came into operation not earlier than 36 months before the installation producing the renewable liquid and gaseous transport fuel of non-biological origin."

This is generally referred to as the **36-month rule**.

### Interpretation

Additionality functions as a **hybrid requirement**: it imposes *capacity eligibility constraints* alongside a strict *volumetric accounting constraint*.

The regulation requires that the renewable electricity be sourced from additional (new) renewable capacity satisfying specific eligibility conditions:

* commissioned no more than 36 months before the electrolyser;
* linked through ownership or qualifying PPAs;
* generally not benefiting from operating support schemes that would already have driven their deployment.

However, the accounting of this electricity is fully **volumetric**. The regulation dictates securing an *amount* (volume) of electricity, meaning the installed renewable capacity required inherently depends on the capacity factors of both the electrolyser and the renewable generator.

---

## 2.2 Interlink Between PPAs and Additionality

A common question is whether PPAs and additionality are the same rule, or if a PPA is strictly required to fulfil additionality. 

**They are two distinct but inextricably linked requirements in a grid-connected setup.** 
* **Additionality** (established in Article 4(2) and Article 5(b)) relates to the *intrinsic attributes* of the generation asset—namely, that the capacity is "new" (the 36-month rule) and unsupported.
* **The PPA** (required by Article 5(a)) is the *contractual vehicle* that serves as the bridge between the RFNBO producer and the additional asset when electricity is drawn from the public grid.

Article 5 sets out these cumulative conditions. To count grid-sourced electricity as fully renewable, the producer must prove:

> "(a) the fuel producers have concluded directly, or via intermediaries, one or more renewables power purchase agreements ... for an amount of renewable electricity that is at least equivalent to the amount of electricity that is claimed as fully renewable;
> 
> (b) the installation generating renewable electricity came into operation not earlier than 36 months before the installation producing the renewable liquid and gaseous transport fuel of non-biological origin..."

Thus, in grid-connected scenarios, the PPA is the mandatory legal mechanism used to claim the volumes produced by an 'additional' installation. 

However, a PPA is **not** absolutely required to fulfil additionality overall. Under **Article 4 (direct line connection)**, where an electrolyser is directly wired to a renewable installation, a PPA is generally not necessary, but the additionality requirement (the 36-month baseline) still strictly applies via Article 4(2)(a).

### Implications

The PPA must cover an amount of renewable generation equivalent to the electricity claimed for RFNBO production (i.e., measured in MWh, not MW of installed capacity).

**Impact of Capacity Factors:**
Because the equivalence is volumetric, satisfying the requirement requires scaling the installed renewable capacity inversely to its capacity factor. For instance, if an electrolyser operates with a 100% capacity factor (8,760 full-load hours/year) and relies on a wind turbine with a 25% capacity factor (2,190 full-load hours/year), maintaining volumetric equivalence requires a **4:1 capacity ratio**. The producer must secure the output of 4 MW of eligible wind capacity to cover the energy consumed by 1 MW of electrolyser capacity over the year (subject strictly to temporal correlation rules).

The legislation therefore requires:

* quantitative volumetric equivalence of renewable generation and claimed consumption;
* compliance with temporal correlation;
* compliance with geographical correlation;
* compliance with the additionality requirements.

---

## 2.3 Support-Scheme Restrictions

A renewable installation generally cannot be counted for additionality if it already receives public support that incentivised its deployment.

The rationale is to avoid double counting of policy support and renewable attributes.

---

# 3. Temporal Correlation

Temporal correlation determines whether renewable electricity generation and hydrogen production occur sufficiently close in time.

---

## 3.1 Current Rule (Until 31 December 2029)

Article 6 provides:

> "Until 31 December 2029 ... the condition of temporal correlation shall be considered complied with if the renewable fuel ... is produced during the same calendar month as the renewable electricity produced under the renewable power purchase agreement."

### Practical Meaning

Current legislation applies:

**Monthly matching**

Hydrogen production in a given month may be matched with renewable generation occurring anywhere within that same month.

Example:

* Wind generation on 1 January.
* Hydrogen production on 28 January.

This remains compliant.

---

## 3.2 Future Rule (From 1 January 2030)

Article 6 states:

> "From 1 January 2030 ... the condition of temporal correlation shall be considered complied with if the renewable fuel ... is produced during the same one-hour period as the renewable electricity."

### Practical Meaning

The framework shifts from:

* monthly matching

to

* hourly matching.

Hydrogen production must be matched hour-by-hour with renewable electricity generation.

---

## 3.3 Storage Treatment

The regulation also permits compliance through storage.

Stored renewable electricity may be counted if:

* the storage is newly installed;
* it is located behind the same grid connection point;
* charging satisfies the temporal matching requirements.

---

# 4. Geographical Correlation

Geographical correlation determines where renewable generation may be located relative to the electrolyser.

---

## 4.1 General Rule

Article 7 requires that renewable electricity be produced:

* in the same bidding zone; or
* in a connected area meeting specified market conditions.

The objective is to avoid situations where renewable generation is physically remote and cannot realistically supply the electrolyser because of transmission constraints.

---

## 4.2 Same Bidding Zone

The default compliance pathway is:

* renewable generator and electrolyser located in the same electricity bidding zone.

This is currently the simplest compliance route.

---

## 4.3 Connected Bidding Zones

Cross-zonal sourcing is permitted when electricity market conditions demonstrate that transmission constraints are not undermining the environmental integrity of the renewable claim.

---

# 5. Existing Exemptions in the Legislation

The Delegated Act contains several important exemptions.

---

## 5.1 Direct Connection Exemption

Where a renewable generator is physically connected to the electrolyser through a direct line:

> "the installations generating renewable electricity are connected to the installation producing renewable liquid and gaseous transport fuel of non-biological origin via a direct line"

In this case:

* electricity can be treated as fully renewable;
* grid-based correlation requirements are largely avoided.

The renewable installation must still satisfy the 36-month commissioning requirement.

---

## 5.2 90% Renewable Electricity Zone Exemption

Grid electricity may be treated as renewable when production occurs in a bidding zone where:

> the share of renewable electricity exceeds 90%.

This is commonly known as the **90% renewable zone exemption**.

Countries frequently discussed as potential beneficiaries include Norway, Iceland and Sweden, depending on annual statistics.

---

## 5.3 Low Carbon Grid Exemption (64.8 gCO₂eq/MJ Threshold)

Hydrogen can also qualify when produced in a bidding zone whose electricity system remains below a prescribed emissions-intensity threshold.

This pathway is often referred to as the:

* defossilised grid pathway;
* low-carbon grid pathway.

It allows hydrogen producers to rely on a sufficiently decarbonised electricity system rather than demonstrating full additionality through PPAs.

---

## 5.4 Low Electricity Price Hours

The Delegated Act also allows RFNBO production during periods of exceptionally low electricity prices.

The rationale is that such periods generally indicate surplus renewable generation and low marginal emissions.

This provision effectively relaxes strict matching requirements during qualifying market conditions.

---

## 5.5 Transitional Exemption for Early Projects

Article 11 provides:

> "Article 5, points (a) and (b) shall not apply until 1 January 2038 to installations ... that come into operation before 1 January 2028."

This is one of the most important provisions for investors.

### Meaning

Projects commissioned before 1 January 2028 receive a grandfathering benefit:

* key additionality requirements remain waived until 2038;
* new capacity added after 2028 does not benefit from this exemption.

---

# 6. Rules Currently Under Review or Political Discussion

These items are **not currently law**.

They arise from the Commission's 2028 review process and ongoing stakeholder debates.

---

## 6.1 Delaying Hourly Matching

Industry stakeholders have proposed:

* extending monthly matching beyond 2030;
* introducing daily matching before hourly matching;
* indefinite monthly matching.

No such amendment has been adopted.

---

## 6.2 Relaxing Additionality

Several proposals would:

* extend the 36-month window;
* allow older renewable assets to qualify;
* partially credit existing renewable generation.

These proposals are under discussion only.

---

## 6.3 Broader Geographic Correlation

Some stakeholders advocate:

* cross-border PPAs over wider regions;
* weaker bidding-zone restrictions.

No legislative change has yet been adopted.

---

## 6.4 Expansion of Defossilised Grid Pathways

Several studies propose:

* lowering the 90% renewable threshold;
* modifying the carbon-intensity threshold;
* expanding eligibility for low-carbon electricity systems.

These remain policy proposals.

# Key Takeaway

The current RFNBO framework is fundamentally built around:

1. **Capacity-based additionality with volumetric equivalence** (new renewable assets must produce an equivalent volume to the amount consumed);
2. **PPA-based accounting**;
3. **Monthly temporal matching until 2030, hourly thereafter**;
4. **Geographical matching through bidding zones**;
5. A set of targeted exemptions (direct connection, highly renewable grids, low-carbon grids, low-price hours, and grandfathering of projects commissioned before 2028).

The largest ongoing political debate concerns whether the post-2030 hourly matching requirement and the current additionality rules are too restrictive for large-scale hydrogen deployment.

A small correction to the literature review you provided: the statement that additionality is purely a "new capacity" requirement is broadly correct on eligibility, but this is always coupled with a **volumetric accounting constraint** (the PPA must cover an amount of renewable generation equivalent to the electricity claimed). Consequently, the installed renewable capacity must scale up based on its capacity factor relative to the electrolyser's capacity factor, as shown in the 4:1 wind-to-electrolyser example. Legally, the Delegated Act is best described as **capacity-based additionality with volumetric equivalence through PPAs**, not exclusively as a capacity rule. ([EUR-Lex][3])
